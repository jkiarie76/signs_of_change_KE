In March 1988, students at Gallaudet University launched a protest after the university selected a hearing person, Elisabeth Ann Zinser, as its president despite two qualified Deaf finalists.

The campus was shut down during a week-long protest. Students, alumni, faculty and staff demanded Deaf leadership and greater Deaf representation in the university's governance.

The movement became known as Deaf President Now, or DPN.

The protesters presented four major demands, including the resignation of the incoming president and the chair of the Board of Trustees, a Board with a Deaf majority, and no reprisals against protesters.

The demands were ultimately met. Dr. I. King Jordan became Gallaudet's first Deaf president, and the movement became a landmark moment in Deaf civil-rights history.

DPN demonstrated the power of organized Deaf leadership and community action. Its influence extended beyond Gallaudet and helped change perceptions about Deaf people's capacity to lead institutions and shape public life.
