Deaf leadership takes many forms.

Some Deaf Kenyans are working in education. Others are building businesses, supporting community organizations, advocating for accessibility or creating new opportunities for younger generations.

Their experiences challenge the idea that Deaf people should simply be recipients of services designed by others.

Instead, Deaf people are increasingly taking active roles in deciding what their communities need and how those needs should be addressed.

Leadership is not always about holding a formal position.

Sometimes it means mentoring another person, organizing a community activity, speaking up for accessibility or creating an opportunity that did not previously exist.

These everyday acts of leadership are helping shape a future in which Deaf people are not only included in society but are also actively helping to define it.
