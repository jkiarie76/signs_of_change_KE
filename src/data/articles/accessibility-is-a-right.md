# Accessibility Is a Right

Accessibility is not a favour or a special privilege. It is an essential part of ensuring that Deaf people can participate fully in society.

## Beyond physical access

Accessibility includes much more than ramps and buildings. Communication access matters too.

Captions, Kenyan Sign Language interpretation, accessible information, visual alerts, and inclusive digital services can make a significant difference.

## Communication access

A Deaf person should not have to fight for basic information simply because that information is delivered only through sound.

Public services, education, healthcare, employment, and digital platforms should consider communication access from the beginning.

## Designing for everyone

When accessibility is considered from the start, everyone benefits. Inclusive design creates environments where people with different communication needs can participate with greater independence.

## A shared responsibility

Accessibility requires action from governments, organisations, businesses, designers, educators, and communities.

Building an accessible Kenya is not simply about compliance. It is about creating a society where everyone belongs.
