# Sign Language and Deaf Identity

For many Deaf people, sign language is much more than a communication tool. It can be an important part of identity, culture, and belonging.

## Language and identity

The language we use can shape how we understand ourselves and how we connect with other people.

For Deaf communities, sign languages provide a shared way to communicate experiences, humour, traditions, ideas, and stories.

## More than communication

Sign language carries culture. Facial expressions, movement, signing space, and visual storytelling all contribute to how meaning is expressed.

## Creating belonging

Access to sign language can give Deaf children and adults opportunities to build relationships with people who understand their experiences.

That sense of belonging can be especially important when Deaf people are surrounded by environments designed primarily around spoken communication.

## Respecting Deaf languages

Learning and using Kenyan Sign Language is one way hearing people can participate more respectfully in Deaf communities.

But respect begins with recognising sign language as a complete language and Deaf people as members of a linguistic and cultural community.
