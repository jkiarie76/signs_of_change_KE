Digital accessibility is changing how workplaces communicate, collaborate and serve their employees.

For Deaf professionals, accessibility is not simply about providing an interpreter during an important meeting. It also involves the everyday systems through which information is shared.

From visual alerts and digital displays to accessible meeting technologies, Kenyan workplaces are beginning to explore new ways of making communication more inclusive.

These changes may seem small, but they can significantly improve independence, participation and belonging.

As more organizations recognize accessibility as part of good workplace design, the conversation is moving beyond compliance toward genuine inclusion.

The future of accessible workplaces will depend not only on technology, but also on whether organizations listen to Deaf people and involve them in designing the solutions they use.
