Laurent Clerc and Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet became two important figures in the early history of Deaf education in the United States.

Gallaudet travelled to Europe while searching for approaches to educating Deaf children. In France, he met Abbé Sicard and two members of his teaching staff, Laurent Clerc and Jean Massieu. Gallaudet eventually invited Clerc to accompany him to the United States.

During their journey, Clerc taught Gallaudet sign language while Gallaudet taught Clerc English. Their partnership led to the establishment of the American School for the Deaf in Hartford, Connecticut, in 1817.

Clerc became the school's first Deaf teacher of Deaf students. His role was significant not simply because he was an educator, but because Deaf people themselves were central participants in the development of Deaf education.

The story of Clerc and Gallaudet is therefore more than the story of two founders. It is also a reminder of the importance of Deaf leadership, language and shared knowledge in building educational institutions.
